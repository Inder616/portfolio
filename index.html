Where hiring gets stuck

Recruitment stalled somewhere between requisition and joined candidate, and nobody could say where. Modelling the funnel across every stage, with turnaround time measured per hiring stage, exposed exactly where candidates were being lost and which recruiters were closing fastest.

Method
  • Built DAX KPIs for requisitions, open positions, applications, shortlists, interviews, offers, selections and joins.
  • Modelled the funnel from requisition through offer, acceptance and joining to isolate drop-off.
  • Measured hiring-stage turnaround time to separate slow stages from slow recruiters.
  • Added slicers for department, location, status, allocated person and date, plus requisition-level detail.

Why patients miss appointments

0

no-show rate once the wait exceeds five days

Clinics were losing revenue to missed appointments they could not predict. Modelling 111K+ booking records isolated waiting time as the dominant driver, ahead of age and gender, and supported a change to scheduling.

Method
  • Processed 111K+ records in Python with feature engineering and data-quality checks to support segmentation.
  • Derived a wait-days field, then bucketed it into delay bands and seven day-range groups.
  • Built no-show rate as a DAX measure so it stayed correct under every slicer.
  • Cross-checked the driver against age group, gender and weekday to confirm delay was not a proxy.

What data roles actually pay

0

median salary for analyst roles worldwide

Job seekers had no single view of pay and required skills across markets. A dynamic Excel dashboard covering 111 countries showed SQL and Python as the dominant skill pair, with senior data scientists earning a 72% premium.

Method
  • Ingested and reshaped the raw postings file through Power Query, handling currency and seniority inconsistencies.
  • Used median rather than mean throughout, since salary data is heavily right-skewed.
  • Split skills into a one-row-per-skill table so combinations could be counted properly.
  • Drove every visual from pivot tables and slicers, so the file refreshes on new data.

Which skills unlock top pay

Layered SQL over a large job-postings table, built to rank roles by pay and then find which skill combinations appear most often in the top decile. The output is a concrete learning order rather than a list.

Method
  • Joined postings to company and skill tables, filtering to remote and named-location roles.
  • Used CTEs to stage the ranking so each step stayed readable and testable.
  • Applied window functions to rank salary within role families rather than across the whole table.
  • Counted skill pairs inside the top decile to find combinations, not just individual demand.
